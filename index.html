Overview

Just the load-bearing facts — the definitions and rules you actually get tested on.

Microbiology

The study of microorganisms — too small to see with the naked eye. Some are harmful (disease), others are useful (industry, recycling of elements).

Medical Microbiology

= Clinical microbiology. Studies pathogenic microbes that cause infectious disease: transmission, portals of entry, virulence, immune response, diagnosis, treatment, prevention & vaccination.

4 Classifications of Microorganisms

  • Viruses
  • Bacteria
  • Fungi, molds & yeasts
  • Protozoa & helminths

Chain of Infection — 6 Links (in order)

  • Infectious Agent
  • Reservoir
  • Portal of Exit
  • Mode of Transmission
  • Portal of Entry
  • Susceptible Host

Susceptibility Depends On

  • A. Genetic factors (e.g. sickle cell trait ↓ malaria risk)
  • B. Nonspecific / Innate immunity (skin, mucosa, cilia, cough reflex)
  • C. Specific immunity (antibodies vs. a specific microbe)

Source of Infection

Endogenous: microbe already in the body (normal flora imbalance) — e.g. E. coli diarrhea.
Exogenous: from outside — carrier, zoonosis, or soil.

Reservoir

Habitat where the agent lives, grows & multiplies: humans, animals, or the environment (e.g. C. botulinum → soil).

Vectors — Know the Difference

Biological: microbe develops inside the vector (malaria in mosquito).
Mechanical: vector just carries it (flies → E. coli / Shigella).

Portals of Entry — Quick List

  • Mouth — ingestion
  • Nose — inhalation
  • Mucous membranes — adhesion
  • Contact — direct / indirect
  • Anal · Vaginal · Placental · Blood

1843–1910Robert KochIdentified anthrax; developed agar medium; Koch's postulates.
1854–1915Paul EhrlichDeveloped chemotherapy; treated sleeping sickness & syphilis.
1881–1951Alexander FlemingDiscovered penicillin & lysozyme.
1864–1920Dmitri IvanovskiDiscovered the first virus — tobacco mosaic virus (TMV).
1952Hershey & ChaseProved DNA is the genetic material of bacteriophages.
